# Fisherman's Island

A pixel-art idle desktop game built in Godot 4.7. Fishermen live on an island, autonomously
walking out to fish, eating, drinking and resting between trips, and are equipped, leveled, and
expanded by the player over time — while the game keeps running, including when it's closed.

## Status

Playable prototype with a full core loop: hire, equip and assign fishermen across four fishing
spots, watch them work through a day/night and seasonal cycle, fill a 204-species fish album, and
spend Scales on permanent upgrades. See [Project structure](#project-structure) below for where
each system lives, or the [wiki](../../wiki) for the deeper design notes.

## Tech stack

- [Godot 4.7](https://godotengine.org/) (GDScript)
- All art and the UI font are generated by small Python scripts in `tools/` rather than
  hand-painted — see [Generated art](#generated-art).

## Running the project

Open this folder as a project in the Godot editor and run it. The `MainMenu` scene is the normal
entry point; `scenes/main/Main.tscn` drops straight into the game.

## Core loop

A fisherman walks to their assigned fishing spot, casts for a randomized duration, and carries the
catch back to the island's shared storage shed. On arrival they either head straight back out or
detour to handle a need — the grill, the beer crate, or a bench — before starting the next trip.
All of it is on screen rather than simulated behind a number.

While fishing, a species and its weight are rolled from the fisherman's Luck and Power, their
gear, the equipped bait, the current weather/season/time of day, and which spot they're fishing.
Common and Uncommon catches auto-sell for Coins; anything Rare or above lands in the **Dock** for
the player to sell for Scales or keep.

Closing the game doesn't pause it: on reload, elapsed real time (capped at 12h, at reduced
efficiency) resolves into a batch of offline catches per fisherman, summarized on a "Welcome back"
screen.

## Systems

- **Fishermen** — a roster of up to 30, each with independently leveled Speed, Luck, Power and
  Endurance, a generated appearance, 1–2 perks rolled at hire, a cosmetic rank derived from their
  catches and levels, and a favourites toggle. They walk in four directions and have distinct
  poses for fishing, eating, drinking and sitting.
- **Needs** — hunger, thirst and rest build up during working time and are serviced at world
  objects. Bench capacity is upgradable, and a fisherman whose bench is taken will handle a
  different need instead of wasting the trip. The grill, beer crate, bench cluster and storage
  shed can all be picked up with right-click and repositioned anywhere on dry land.
- **Fishing spots** — pond, pier and offshore jetty. Each reaches a different slice of the fish
  catalog, from Common-through-Epic at the pond up to Rare-and-above offshore. The pier and jetty
  are meta-shop unlocks; the jetty is only drawn once bought.
- **Gear** — 365 items generated from 31 families across 5 slots (Rod/Hat/Outfit/Charm/Bait).
  Conditions are live effects rather than shop filters, so a storm rod really is better in a
  storm, and bait steers *which* species surface. See the [wiki](../../wiki/Gear).
- **Fish** — 204 hand-authored species (a mix of invented and real-world fish, including several
  marine mammals and a sea turtle) across 13 habitats and 7 tiers, including a hidden Secret tier.
  See the [wiki](../../wiki/Fish).
- **The world clock** — nested day/night, weather, and season cycles that bias stats and gate both
  species and gear bonuses.
- **Meta-progression** — 17 permanent upgrades paid for in Scales: the two fishing-spot unlocks,
  global Speed/Luck/Power/Endurance, shop and hire discounts, Coin gain, offline efficiency, bench
  and dock capacity, needs service time, shop rarity odds, an extra perk slot, and a Secret catch
  bonus that stays hidden until the first Secret fish is caught.
- **Album** — a discovery log for every species, browsable by rarity tier or by habitat, with
  per-species stats, flavour text, and a record holder for the heaviest catch of each.
- **Developer console** (`~` or F1, debug builds only) — fast-forwards time, forces weather, grants
  currency, forces a catch of a given rarity, and simulates an offline session, so nothing above
  needs real minutes to test.

## Generated art

Everything visual is produced by Python scripts under `tools/` using
[Pillow](https://pillow.readthedocs.io/), from readable source data rather than painted by hand:

- `generate_fisherman_sprites.py` builds one 144×72 sheet per appearance variant (a 9×3 grid of
  16×24 frames) from ASCII pixel maps and a per-variant palette. Columns are stand, two walk
  poses, two fishing poses, a strike, two eating/drinking poses and sitting; rows are the down,
  right and up facings, with "left" mirrored at runtime.
- `generate_fish_sprites.py` builds two aligned atlases (grid size grows with the model count,
  see `COLUMNS`/`ROWS` in the script): a full-colour body and a separate white outline mask. The
  Album tints the *mask* with a fish's rarity colour, so the generated body colours stay clean.
- `generate_pixel_font.py` builds the UI font — a 108-glyph bitmap font covering ASCII plus the
  symbols the interface actually uses. It only looks right at its native 10px or an exact integer
  multiple, which is why every font size in the project is 10, 20 or 30.

Re-run a script after editing it to rebake its output. The world itself, the menu backdrop and the
UI icons aren't assets at all — they're drawn procedurally as whole-pixel rects.

## Project structure

- `scenes/` — Godot scenes (`main/` the game itself, `entities/` fisherman and station, `ui/`
  shared components, `main_menu/`)
- `scripts/` — GDScript source: autoload singletons for global state (`economy.gd`,
  `world_clock.gd`, `album.gd`, `dock_inventory.gd`, `meta_progress.gd`, `need_stations.gd`, ...),
  static catalogs (`fish_catalog.gd`, `gear_families.gd`, `fishing_spots.gd`, `world_layout.gd`),
  and per-scene controllers alongside the scenes that use them
- `assets/` — generated sprites, the pixel font, and the UI theme
- `tools/` — the Python generators above

## Saving

Autosaves every 30 seconds and on menu/quit, to Godot's user data directory
(`user://savegame.json`). One save per install; there's no slot management yet.
